# TrustNet Evidence Source Hierarchy

This hierarchy guides retrieval, reranking, provenance assessment, and evidence selection.

It is a preference framework, not an automatic truth ranking.

## Tier 1 — Primary and Authoritative Sources

Prefer these whenever directly relevant.

Examples:
- Government agencies and official public records
- Courts, statutes, regulations, and official legal documents
- Peer-reviewed research and original scientific papers
- Official datasets and statistical agencies
- Regulatory filings
- Official reports from recognized institutions
- Direct statements or records from the relevant organization

These sources usually provide the strongest provenance because the information can be traced close to its origin.

## Tier 2 — High-Quality Independent Secondary Sources

Examples:
- Established news agencies
- Reputable investigative journalism
- Systematic reviews and evidence syntheses
- Established fact-checking organizations
- Academic or institutional analysis

Use these for corroboration, interpretation, and cases where primary evidence is unavailable.

## Tier 3 — General Secondary Sources

Examples:
- General news websites
- Specialist publications
- Professional blogs
- Educational websites
- Industry publications

These may provide useful evidence but should preferably be corroborated by stronger sources.

## Tier 4 — Weak or Unverified Sources

Examples:
- Anonymous websites
- Unsourced blogs
- Social-media posts
- Forums
- Content aggregators
- Unverified user-generated content

These may be useful for identifying a claim or tracing where it originated, but should normally not be treated as decisive evidence without independent corroboration.

# Core Rules

1. Source tier alone does not determine whether a claim is true.

2. Evidence must directly address the claim.

3. Primary evidence should generally be preferred over reporting about the primary evidence.

4. Multiple independent sources are preferable to multiple sources repeating the same original report.

5. Recency matters when the claim is time-sensitive.

6. Older evidence may remain appropriate for historical or stable claims.

7. A high-quality source discussing an unrelated issue is not useful evidence.

8. TrustNet must distinguish the source making the original claim from sources used to verify that claim.

9. Conflicts between strong sources must be preserved and reported rather than hidden.

10. No source should receive automatic credibility solely because its domain appears on a whitelist.

11. Retrieved URLs must correspond to the actual evidence presented to the user.

12. Evidence quality, relevance, independence, provenance, and temporal appropriateness should all influence evidence selection.
